Roibase has operated remotely since 2019 — but shifted to a "hub-first" model starting in 2024. 60% of the team is concentrated in 5 cities: Istanbul, Lisbon, Berlin, Mexico City, and Bangkok. This distribution isn't random — each hub was selected based on balancing taxation, infrastructure, timezone, cost, and legal frameworks. The evaluation below is grounded in team data-driven operational reports, not personal experience.

## Istanbul — Primary Hub and Async Center

Istanbul is Roibase's main hub. 35% of the team works here, with the operational center at UTC+3. Europe sits at +0 to +2 hour offset, Asia at -2 to -4 — this overlap window provides 6 hours of synchronous collaboration daily.

Internet infrastructure: TurkNet fiber delivers 1000/100 Mbps — median ping to EU servers is 35ms, Cloudflare Istanbul edge 8ms. Coworking costs €150–250/month (Kolektif House, Workinton). The legal stack is complex: foreign corporate tax is 20%, but withholding and VAT structures require a consultant. The 90-day tourist visa isn't sufficient — "worker" status is required.

Cost breakdown: 1-bedroom average €500–700/month (Kadıköy, Beşiktaş), meals €3–6, transportation €40/month. Livable for tech teams, but bureaucratic processes are slow. Roibase conducts [branding initiatives](https://www.roibase.com.tr/ru/branding) from here — local culture reinforces brand consistency.

### Strengths and Weaknesses

Istanbul's real strength is timezone. UTC+3 bridges both European and Asian teams — in an async culture, this serves a critical "bridge" function. The disadvantage is legal complexity: work permits take 3–6 months to obtain, and corporate setup is difficult without a consultant.

Team rotation: junior developers spend 6 months in Istanbul, then 6 months in another hub — this is critical for culture transfer. Turkey's tech ecosystem is growing rapidly, but still operates in a "corporate-first" structure — remote-friendly regulation is weak.

## Lisbon — EU Legal Base and Low Visa Barriers

Lisbon is Roibase's legal headquarters in the EU. 20% of the team is based here, and the Portuguese corporate entity is registered here. The NHR (Non-Habitual Resident) scheme closed in 2024, but the D7 visa remains attractive — show €800/month passive income and obtain a residence permit in 2 years.

Internet: MEO fiber delivers 1000/200 Mbps, median ping to EU cloud 12ms. Coworking costs €200–300/month (Second Home, Selina Sea). The legal framework is straightforward: freelance operation takes 2 weeks, corporate setup 4 weeks. Corporate tax is 21%, with R&D incentives available.

Cost: 1-bedroom €900–1,200/month (Alfama, Santos), meals €8–12, transportation €40/month (metro + tram). 40% more expensive than Istanbul, but mid-range within the EU.

Timezone disadvantage: UTC+0, only 2–3 hours overlap with Asia teams. Async culture becomes mandatory — real-time meeting count drops 30%.

### Legal Advantage

Lisbon's real value is EU passport strength. A Portuguese residence permit unlocks unlimited Schengen access. If the team includes members from non-Schengen countries (US, UK, Singapore), Lisbon serves as a legal "safe zone." Roibase's corporate entity can service all 27 EU countries from here — the VAT MOSS system is straightforward.

## Berlin — High Cost, High Regulation

Berlin is a pilot hub — 10% of the team, rotating rather than permanent. Germany's freelance visa (Freiberufler) takes 3 months with a 90% approval rate. Corporate setup takes 8 weeks — the Finanzamt (tax authority) is slow.

Internet: Telekom fiber delivers 1000/200 Mbps, median ping to EU cloud 8ms. Coworking costs €300–400/month (Factory Berlin, Mindspace). Costs: 1-bedroom €1,200–1,500/month (Kreuzberg, Prenzlauer Berg), meals €10–15, transportation €49/month (Deutschlandticket).

Timezone UTC+1, one hour ahead of Lisbon. The overlap window with the rest of the team is narrow — 4–5 hours daily. Async communication is 50% more intensive here.

### Why Berlin?

Berlin appears as "high cost, low ROI" for the team — but legal security is exceptionally strong. Germany offers tech teams the best social security net (healthcare, pension). Roibase rotates senior developers through Berlin for 3–6 months — for skill transfer and legal backup.

Berlin's disadvantage is bureaucracy. Anmeldung (address registration) takes 4–6 weeks, opening a bank account 2 weeks. But once setup is complete, the process stabilizes.

## Mexico City — Western Hemisphere Bridge

Mexico City launched in 2025. 15% of the team is based here, in the UTC-6 timezone. US west coast sits at +0 offset, east coast at +3 — this enables Roibase's real-time collaboration with US clients.

Internet: Telmex fiber delivers 500/50 Mbps, median ping to US cloud 40ms. Coworking costs €150–200/month (WeWork, Terminal 1). Legal framework: 180-day tourist visa available, but official work permits are complicated. Most team members operate under "digital nomad" status — a gray area.

Cost: 1-bedroom €600–800/month (Roma Norte, Condesa), meals €4–8, transportation €20/month (metro + metrobus). Cost parity with Istanbul, but internet infrastructure is weaker.

### Timezone Strategy

Mexico City's value is timezone. UTC-6 provides 2–3 hours daily overlap with both Europe and Asia, but 8 hours with US clients. Roibase's client-facing team works from here — real-time meetings are 60% easier.

The disadvantage is legal uncertainty. Mexico hasn't granted official remote work status; tax residency is complicated. Stay longer than 6 months and you're classified as tax resident, but enforcement is weak.

## Bangkok — Asia Hub and Low Cost

Bangkok is Roibase's Asia hub. 20% of the team is based here, in UTC+7. Singapore, Hong Kong, and Tokyo sit at +0 to +2 hour offset — synchronous work with the Asia-Pacific team is seamless.

Internet: True fiber delivers 1000/500 Mbps, median ping to APAC cloud 15ms. Coworking costs €100–150/month (AIS D.C., The Hive). Legal framework: 30-day tourist visa (extendable another 30 days), LTR (Long-Term Resident) visa 10 years — but requires $80k/year income.

Cost: 1-bedroom €400–600/month (Sukhumvit, Ari), meals €2–5, transportation €30/month (BTS + MRT). The most affordable of Roibase's hubs.

### Asia-Pacific Strategy

Bangkok's strength is the cost-infrastructure balance. Internet speed matches Istanbul's, but costs are 30% lower. 8-hour daily overlap with Asia teams — critical for real-time collaboration.

The disadvantage is timezone isolation. -5 hours from Europe, -13 hours from the US — async culture is mandatory. Roibase's Bangkok rotations run 3–6 months — longer stints fragment team culture.

Roibase's hub strategy doesn't hunt for a single "best city" — each hub addresses distinct operational needs. Istanbul serves as the async bridge, Lisbon as legal base, Berlin as senior backup, Mexico City for US client-facing work, Bangkok as the Asia-Pacific hub. Team rotations — members spend 3–6 months in each hub — ensure skill transfer across all locations.

This model isn't "nomadic" but "distributed-yet-anchored" — infrastructure is built in each hub, legal entities are maintained. In 2026, new hub evaluations are underway: Dubai (tax, Asia-EU bridge), Tallinn (e-residency, low-touch corporate). The criteria remain constant: timezone overlap, legal clarity, infrastructure reliability, cost-value balance.
