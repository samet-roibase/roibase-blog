Roibase was founded in Istanbul in 2019, but shifted to a distributed team model starting in 2022. Today, 27 people work across 5 main hubs: Istanbul (13), Lisbon (6), Berlin (4), Mexico City (2), Bangkok (2). This choice was not accidental — each city meets specific operational criteria. In this article, we share concrete hub evaluations from Roibase's 5 years of remote work experience: internet speed, coworking costs, time zone overlap, visa duration, team scaling ease, and cost per bandwidth. This is not a generic "digital nomad guide" — it's an operational reference for tech companies building remote engineering teams.

## Istanbul: Headquarters and Cost Efficiency

Istanbul is Roibase's founding location and still has the largest team concentration. 13 people are distributed across the Maslak, Levent, and Kadıköy triangle. Fiber internet has 98% uptime with average 100 Mbps download (Superonline, Türk Telekom fiber). Coworking costs ₺8,000–12,000 per month (2026 prices), roughly $220 per person. UTC+3 time zone offers 09:00–13:00 overlap with Europe, completely asynchronous (16-hour gap) with US West Coast.

Operational advantage: high-quality frontend, backend, and data engineer pool. Mid-to-senior developer salaries in Istanbul are 60% of Berlin's, 75% of Lisbon's. No visa required for Turkish citizens; foreign workers need a work permit (60–90 day process). Main challenge: TL volatility — salary payments must be pegged to dollar parity, otherwise 30%+ losses occur within 6 months.

Istanbul's second advantage is time zone efficiency. European mornings (09:00–11:00 CET) overlap with Istanbul midday (11:00–13:00 TRT) — critical for synchronous tasks like standups and sprint planning. Mexico City and Bangkok have zero overlap; these cities operate entirely on async protocols.

## Lisbon: European Hub and Legal Simplicity

Lisbon was the first expansion point in 2022. 6 people in Parque das Nações and Cais do Sodré. Fiber averages 500 Mbps (MEO, Vodafone), 99%+ uptime. Coworking costs €300–450/month per person (Selina, Second Home, Heden). UTC+0 time zone — Europe's primary time, +3 hours from Istanbul. Lisbon-Istanbul overlap is 09:00–17:00 Lisbon = 12:00–20:00 Istanbul. 8-hour window — ideal for team coordination.

Legal framework is straightforward: D7 visa (passive income) issues within 6 months, renewable after 12 months. Work visa requires company registration — LDA setup takes 2–3 weeks, starting cost €1,500–2,000. Portugal's NHR (Non-Habitual Resident) tax regime narrowed in 2024 but still offers 20% flat rate for first 5 years (certain professions).

Lisbon's weakness: narrow skilled pool. Finding a senior backend developer can take 4–6 months. Suitable for remote team scaling but limited for local hiring. Of Roibase's 6 Lisbon team members, 4 relocated from Istanbul, 2 from elsewhere in Europe.

## Berlin: Infrastructure and Regulatory Balance

Berlin is the third hub with 4 people, distributed across Kreuzberg and Mitte. 1 Gbps fiber is common (Deutsche Telekom, Vodafone), 99.5%+ uptime. Coworking €400–600/month per person (Ahoy, Unicorn, Betahaus). UTC+1, +2 hours from Istanbul. Overlap window is 08:00–17:00 Berlin = 10:00–19:00 Istanbul. 9-hour window — comparable to Lisbon.

Berlin's advantage is infrastructure quality and regulatory clarity. Work visa (Freiberufler or GmbH-based) takes 8–12 weeks with predictable process. Berlin team is critical for GDPR compliance — data governance, cookie consent, and first-party data architecture are managed from the European side. Roibase's [Branding & Brand Identity](https://www.roibase.com.tr/ru/branding) work incorporates GDPR-ready design with Berlin team input.

Berlin's weakness is cost. Mid-to-senior frontend developer salaries run €75,000–90,000 annually, 2.5x Istanbul's. Coworking plus living costs total €3,500–4,000/month per person. Operationally, Berlin is reserved for compliance-critical roles and European customer management. General development comes from Istanbul and Lisbon.

## Mexico City: Latam Time Zone and Talent Pool

Mexico City opened in 2025 with 2 people in Roma Norte and Condesa. 200 Mbps fiber (Telmex, Totalplay), 95–97% uptime (occasional outages; backup 5G recommended). Coworking $250–400/month (WeWork, Terminal 1). UTC-6 (winter) / UTC-5 (summer), 8–9 hours from Istanbul. Overlap is 14:00–17:00 Mexico City = 22:00–01:00 Istanbul — minimal. Mexico City operates entirely on async protocols: Loom videos, async standups, documentation-first culture.

Mexico City's strategic value is Latam time zone coverage. US West Coast customers (09:00–17:00 PST) align with 11:00–19:00 Mexico City. While Istanbul sleeps, Mexico City provides US-hours support. Second advantage: Spanish-speaking developer pool. Latam customers benefit from local language and cultural context — Mexico City team closes this gap.

Weakness: visa uncertainty. Temporary Resident Visa covers 6–12 months but renewal is uncertain each cycle. No long-term stability. Roibase plans to scale Mexico City to 4–6 people but visa risk slows progress.

## Bangkok: Cost and Asia Gateway

Bangkok is the newest hub with 2 people in Sukhumvit and Ari. 1 Gbps fiber (True, 3BB), 98%+ uptime. Coworking ฿8,000–12,000/month ($220–330, Hubba, The Hive). UTC+7, +4 hours from Istanbul (summer), +5 (winter). Overlap is 12:00–16:00 Bangkok = 08:00–12:00 Istanbul. 4-hour window — narrow but manageable.

Bangkok's primary advantage is cost. Mid-to-senior developer salaries run $35,000–45,000 annually, 70% of Istanbul's. Living costs $1,200–1,800/month (including coworking). Cost per bandwidth — lowest among hubs. Second advantage: Asia-Pacific time zone coverage. Singapore, Tokyo, Sydney customers align with 09:00–17:00 Bangkok time.

Weakness: variable talent pool quality. Finding high-caliber Thai developers is difficult — most work for Singapore firms or remote companies. Both Roibase Bangkok team members relocated from Turkey. No local hiring success yet. Visa: DTV (Destination Thailand Visa) is 5-year, remote-worker friendly, 4–8 week application.

## Operational Conclusion and Next 12 Months

The 5-hub model delivers 3 critical benefits for Roibase: (1) **24-hour coverage** — the Istanbul-Bangkok-Mexico City triangle ensures one team is active every hour. (2) **Time zone arbitrage** — expensive work in Istanbul/Berlin, operational support in Bangkok. (3) **Regulatory hedge** — if one country tightens visa/tax policy, shift to another hub.

For H2 2026: scale Mexico City to 4 people, attempt local hiring in Bangkok, expand Lisbon to 8. Berlin stays fixed (compliance-focused roles). Istanbul team expected to grow from 13 to 15, but growth slower than other hubs due to Turkish macroeconomic uncertainty.

For tech companies building remote teams: start with one hub, test for 12 months, then add a second. Managing 3+ hubs simultaneously creates operational friction — don't expand before async protocols, documentation discipline, and tool stack are solid. Roibase took 3 years to reach 5 hubs. First 18 months were Istanbul + Lisbon only, system stabilized, then Berlin, Mexico City, Bangkok added sequentially. Prioritize sustainability over speed. Roibase's hub strategy wasn't venture-capital growth; it was operational resilience.
